# How Themezer-3DS's banner, boot animation and music were made

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `meta/banner.cgfx` | the 3D banner on the HOME Menu |
| `meta/audio.wav` | the tune the HOME Menu plays with the banner |
| `meta/logo.bin` | the boot animation shown while the app starts |
| `romfs/bgm.bcstm` | the background music in the app |

All four were generated by scripts that are not in this repository. The steps, the sources and every setting that
mattered are below, so they can be made again.

## Banner: `meta/banner.cgfx`

A CGFX model: the Themezer controller and brush as a slab with rounded edges, and below it the "Themezer" wordmark on
a flat plane that always faces the camera.

1. **Model.** The logo comes from the icon in the Themezer design files (`design/icon.svg`, rendered to PNG). Its
   outline becomes the slab's shape. The edge is rounded like a pill: two quarter rounds, the icon's outline width
   each, with a flat band of 0.55 units between them. The front shows the icon's art inside a white rim. The d-pad
   and the four buttons stand 0.35 units out of the front face: white, with rounded edges of their own (0.1 units),
   their footprint the art's shapes and their backs inside the slab. The back face shows the icon rendered without
   its d-pad and buttons, just the gradient and the brush, since a controller's back has no buttons. The wordmark
   comes from `design/logotext.svg`: a white layer (the letters grown by 2.3 texels, for the outline) and a
   fill layer on top. Each is a white-on-transparent 256x64 texture with the text in the middle 75 %.
2. **Scene.** The logo sits in the `world` node, which the HOME Menu turns itself in response to the microphone and
   motion. The HOME Menu turns it clockwise seen from above. The logo's own node starts turned 45° the other way, so it
   comes round to face the viewer after 45° instead of turning away at once. The wordmark's node is a Y-axis
   billboard. Layout is in the HOME Menu's banner units: the camera is at
   (0, 1, 44.8) with a 30° field of view, and everything stays between y = −8 and 9.5, clear of the status bar and
   the hints. Everything is written out as glTF.
3. **Conversion.** [pycgfx](https://github.com/skyfloogle/pycgfx) (commit `1f78850`) converts the glTF, and the
   result is then patched:
   - each mesh is bound to its node by name;
   - the materials are matte (pycgfx's specular term washes the banner out under the HOME Menu's light);
   - the logo gets a lighting floor: 60 % of its texture mixed back in, so the rounded edge doesn't go dark;
   - the wordmark is unlit, drawn straight from its texture;
   - the wordmark's fill takes the icon's gradient (`#ff00ce` to `#00d079`) from vertex colours, 8 columns across
     the text; the colours get a 1.25× chroma boost in OKLab, because the 3DS screen washes them out;
   - the logo art is ETC1A4 (RGBA4 bands the gradient; plain ETC1 comes out scrambled on the HOME Menu);
   - the wordmark textures are LA8, filtered linearly both ways (texture parameters `0x6`), with the texture
     coordinates zoomed to the middle 75 %, so the letters are drawn larger than their texture and come out smooth;
   - each sampler gets the LOD bias that pycgfx leaves out of the record;
   - **every texture's data starts on a 128-byte boundary of the file.** pycgfx uses 16, and with that the HOME Menu
     hangs (GPU waits forever) as soon as it filters a texture linearly. This was the long-standing banner freeze.
4. **Banner file.** The Makefile runs `bannertool makebanner -ci meta/banner.cgfx -a meta/audio.wav`. A CGFX must
   stay under 512 KB.

## Banner tune: `meta/audio.wav`

Synthesized from scratch in Python (numpy). No samples were used. It is "bloom-original-harp": the "bloom" phrase on a
harp, at its written pitch.

- **Phrase:** D major at 112 bpm.
  1. A rising Gmaj9 arpeggio (B4, D5, F♯5, A5) over G2 and D3.
  2. E6, D6, C♯6 over A, a suspension falling to the leading note.
  3. A rolled D(add9) chord from D2 up, with F♯6 on top.
  4. A soft A6.
- **Harp:** each note is a plucked string, with harmonics weighted by where it is plucked (a fifth of the way along).
  The upper harmonics die out much faster than the fundamental, and high notes ring shorter than low ones. A tiny
  burst of filtered noise gives the pluck. Harder notes are brighter, and the strings are damped at each chord change.
- **Echo:** an eighth-note ping-pong delay, right first, with each repeat darker and quieter. On top of that, a small
  room: a decaying-noise impulse response that gets darker as it fades.
- **Ending:** the phrase is over by about 1.2 s; the rest is the notes and echoes ringing out, followed by a slow
  fade.
- **Format:** 2.95 s, 44.1 kHz, stereo, 16-bit. The HOME Menu plays about 3 seconds at most.

## Boot animation: `meta/logo.bin`

Written from scratch after the Homebrew App Store icon (`design/hbappstore/icon.svg`): its chevron background moving
up on both screens, the Themezer logo and wordmark over it on the top screen, fading in from black and out to black.

1. **Art.** The icon SVG was split into its two layers and each rendered at 2560x1500:
   - **Background (`BG` layer):** `#292929`, overlaid with the "Wave 2" pattern in `#1a1a1a`. The pattern cell is
     30 × 9 units, which is 55.14 × 16.54 icon pixels.
   - **Foreground (`Layer 1` layer):** the logo and the wordmark.

   Everything is placed at 1.6 times the icon's size (the 150-pixel icon on the 240-pixel top screen).

2. **Textures,** all BCLIM in A4 (alpha only), at one texel per screen pixel:
   - the logo's silhouette, 105 × 109;
   - the logo's coloured fill, 105 × 109, in its own texture with an empty border;
   - the wordmark, 156 × 41;
   - one chevron cell, 64 × 32, repeated across the screen;
   - a solid 8 × 8 white square.

   Each pane is exactly its texture's size, with its edges on pixel edges, so nothing is resampled.

3. **Colour.** An A4 texture has no colour of its own. Every material uses the three-stage TEV copied from Nintendo's
   logo: texture, then the black/white colour interpolation, then multiplied by the vertex colours. With the black
   colour set to white, a mask draws in its vertex colours. The logo's gradient is the SVG's own:
   - `#ff00ce` to `#00d079`, from (88.87, 146.28) to (189.62, 206.24) in the icons layer, solid past either end;
   - four corner colours can't make that, so the fill is drawn as three quads rotated across the gradient (solid
     magenta, the blend, solid green), each taking its part of the fill mask through its texture coordinates;
   - the white silhouette sits underneath and shows through the plus sign and the buttons.

4. **Layouts and animations.** BCLYT and BCLAN, CTR layout version 2.2.0.0, written by our own writer. The writer
   rebuilds Nintendo's logo files byte for byte. The HOME Menu looks only for these names, never for pane names:
   - the layouts `blyt/NintendoLogo_U_00.bclyt` (top screen, 400×240) and `blyt/NintendoLogo_D_00.bclyt` (bottom
     screen, 320×240);
   - the animations `anim/NintendoLogo_{U,D}_00_SceneOut{A,B,C}.bclan`;
   - the groups `G_A_00`, `G_B_00` and `G_C_00`.

   The animations:

   | Animation | Length | Role |
   |---|---|---|
   | A | 60 frames | the intro |
   | B | 40 frames | loops until the app has started |
   | C | 15 frames | the fade-out |

   - **Chevrons:** they move up one cell (26.5 pixels) every 40 frames. A runs from half a cell below to one cell
     up, so B carries on without a jump.
   - **Fades:** a black full-screen pane over everything does both. It fades out over A's first 10 frames and back
     in over C. The logo itself is never faded, because fading it lets its white layer show through the colours.

5. **Packing.** The files go into a DARC archive:
   - every file starts on a 4-byte boundary (the HOME Menu reads the layouts' words and floats in place), and
     textures on 128-byte ones;
   - after the archive comes a 32-byte HMAC-SHA256 of the archive (see below);
   - the whole is LZ11-compressed with an optimal parse and padded to exactly `0x2000` bytes. It is 32 KB
     uncompressed and 5.3 KB compressed.

   **The HMAC trailer is required.** The HOME Menu checks it before showing the animation and panics at launch on a
   mismatch, so it must be recomputed after any edit. The key is 32 bytes from the HOME Menu's own memory (at
   `0x306465` in the EUR HOME Menu, read with Rosalina's gdb). It is kept out of this repository.

   makerom's built-in Homebrew logo confirmed which choices are safe. It uses its own pane names, decompresses to
   59 KB, and its waves use the same repeat wrap mode (`5`).

## Background music: `romfs/bgm.bcstm`

"Aether", generated with Suno: 2:14 long, at 133 bpm. It loops a 16-bar stretch of the calm first part, never
reaching the louder part after 46.85 s.

1. **Finding the loop.** The song's beats were tracked (spectral-flux onsets, tempo by autocorrelation, beats by
   dynamic programming). For every pair of beats a whole number of bars apart, the music around the two points was
   compared by harmony (chroma) and timbre (log-mel). The loop runs from **16.20 s to 45.06 s**:
   - 45.06 s is where the calm part ends with a break, just before the louder part starts;
   - 16.20 s is one bar before the next section starts;
   - the sound just before the two points matches 0.97 out of 1, and the length is exactly 16 bars (28.86 s), so the
     phrasing stays even. Loops of 12 and 13 bars sounded like a section was missing.
2. **The join.** The loop end was shifted by up to ±5 ms to where the waveforms line up best. A larger shift would
   change the loop's length enough to put the beat off. The last beat before the end is then crossfaded (equal power,
   one beat long) into the beat before the start, and that crossfade is baked into the audio.
3. **Resampling.** The audio was converted to 32,728 Hz, the DSP's own rate, with ffmpeg's Kaiser-windowed resampler.
   Before resampling, the music after the loop start is appended past the loop end, so the resampler sees what really
   follows the end.
4. **Encoding.** The Themezer webapp's own encoder (`encodePcmToBcstm` in themezer-monorepo
   `packages/3dstheme-builder`) writes a looping DSP-ADPCM BCSTM at about 37 dB SNR.
   - **Block-aligned loop start:** the encoder moves the loop start forward to the next block boundary (14,336
     samples; here by 238 samples) and appends that much of the loop after the end, so the loop's music is unchanged.
     This matters because the app's BCSTM player jumps back by whole blocks.
   - **Result:** 1.69 MB, 45.06 s, a loop of 28.856 s.
   - **Checks:** the loop start is on block 37, and each channel's loop context equals the decoded history at that
     point.
5. **Playback.** The app plays it quietly in the background, on sound channels of its own, and pauses it while a
   theme's music preview plays. It can be turned off in the app's settings.
